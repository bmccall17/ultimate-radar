## The referee test is not the one the M0 review predicted

The M0 review, and `HANDOFF.md` after it, said the appearance half of AD-3 would
be torso luminance **variance**: stripes have it, a flat kit does not. That is
true and it is not sufficient, because two other things on this footage have high
torso variance too:

- a Wind Chill jersey carries a large light number on the back;
- a box drawn round two overlapping players contains one dark torso and one light
  one.

Both look exactly like stripes to a variance test. Measured over the 20 held-out
frames, variance does not separate referees from either.

What does separate them is that stripes are **periodic and vertically coherent**,
and neither a number nor a neighbour is. So:

1. Average luminance **down** the torso. Vertical stripes are coherent down the
   body and survive the average; a number's contrast and a neighbour's shoulder
   largely do not.
2. **High-pass** that column profile by subtracting a moving average. A
   neighbouring light player is a low-frequency ramp across the patch; stripes are
   high-frequency.
3. Count the runs that stay bright.

Three or more bright runs caught **5 of the 8 referees and none of the 234
players or 15 crew**. That is a high-precision, moderate-recall test and it is
reported that way. Two attempts to raise the recall were tried and rejected
because they cost precision:

| Variant | Referees caught | Real players wrongly flagged |
|---|---|---|
| One fixed torso band (kept) | 5 / 8 | **0 / 234** |
| Best of eight bands | 6 / 8 | 15 / 234 |
| Best of two bands | 5 / 8 | 1 / 234 |
| Band shifted down the torso | 4 / 8 | 0 / 234 |

Scanning several bands and taking the best is a multiple-comparisons trap: more
chances to find structure that is not there. One band, measured, is the honest
choice.

The three referees it misses are turned or small, so the stripes foreshorten out
of the column average. **They survive as Wind Chill players**, and the effect is
visible in `eval/m3/p0001_teams.mp4`: frame 60 reports *8 Wind Chill* in a
seven-a-side point, and detection #4 there is a referee. They carry `weak_team`
and a low `team_score` so that nothing downstream can mistake them for confirmed
players. **M4 is where this closes properly** — a referee whose stripes fail one
frame's pose is still a referee across a tracklet, and a vote over a track settles
it. 8.7 % of kept detections (349 of 3990) sit in the neither-kit band; that is
the upper bound on the leak, and it includes contaminated two-player boxes as well
as referees.

## Crew: position **and** appearance, because neither alone is safe

AD-3 specifies the reject as position *and* appearance. Both halves earn their
place here:

- **Position alone** means shrinking the out-of-bounds margin. In ultimate a
  defender chasing a throw over the line and a thrower with a pivot on the line
  are both out of play and both matter, so that would throw away real players.
- **Appearance alone** risks a mid-grey reading on a real player.

Together — torso matching neither kit *and* feet beyond the far sideline — catches
**12 of 15** crew and cannot touch a player: the six players whose torsos do land
between the kits are all at soccer y = −2 to 0, mid-pitch, while 13 of the 15 crew
sit at y ≥ 21.0 against a far sideline at 20.58.

Honest limit: no labelled player in this possession is beyond the far sideline, so
the appearance half of this rule is doing no *measurable* work here. Position alone
would score the same on this data. It is kept because it is what makes the rule
safe the first time a player does step out, and this possession never tests that.

## The kits are fitted, not hard-coded

Torso luminance over every in-bounds detection, split by a deterministic 2-means
seeded at the extremes. On p0001: dark **L\* 40.9**, light **L\* 82.8**,
separation **41.8**, fitted on 4198 torsos. No constant in the module names a
colour, so a different venue, a different time of day or a different pair of kits
needs nothing edited.

The fit re-checks its own premise. Below 20 L\* of separation the module says
plainly that the two kits are not separable by lightness on that footage and that
every team call it makes is unsafe — rather than splitting one cluster in half and
reporting two teams.

**Confusion, 260 labelled in-bounds boxes:**

| truth ↓ / called → | sol | chill | ref | crew |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sol (116) | **116** | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| chill (118) | 1 | **117** | 0 | 0 |
| ref (8) | 0 | 3 | **5** | 0 |
| crew (15) | 0 | 3 | 0 | **12** |
| unsure (3, excluded) | 2 | 1 | 0 | 0 |

The single team error is f245 #3, a box containing a Chill player and a Sol player
overlapping, whose torso sample is contaminated by the Sol jersey.

## The sigma was 46 % honest, and that matters more than the gate

M2 stated `sigma_yd` as an **assumed** 3 px of foot error times the local
ground-plane scale, and said so. Measured on 50 hand-checked detections:

| | |
|---|---|
| Median foot-point error | **0.4345 yd** (gate < 0.6) |
| p95 / max | 1.1485 / 3.4225 yd |
| Pixel offset, median / rms | 4.12 / **5.83 px** (M2 assumed 3.0) |
| Systematic bias | dx −1.52 px, dy −1.66 px |
| Truths inside the *old* sigma disc | **46 %** |
| Truths inside the corrected disc | **94 %** |

`docs/05-uncertainty.md` requires ≥ 80 % of truths inside the drawn sigma, and
says why: the viewer's whole honesty argument is that the disc contains the truth.
At 46 % the disc was not a conservative estimate, it was a claim that happened to
be wrong more often than not — the same shape as the four bugs in `HANDOFF.md`
§ 7, something reporting success while being wrong.

`FOOT_UNCERTAINTY_PX` is now **5.83**, the rms of the measurement. 4.08 px would
hit exactly 80 %; the rms is preferred because it is a definition rather than a
number reverse-engineered from the gate it has to pass.

Error grows with distance the way the model says it should:

| Band | yd per px | Median error | p95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| near | 0.099–0.133 | 0.354 yd | 1.140 |
| middle | 0.133–0.169 | 0.364 yd | 0.579 |
| far | 0.185–0.277 | 0.758 yd | 1.771 |

Two things measured and deliberately **not** acted on:

- **The bias.** The true foot sits 1.5 px left of and 1.7 px above the box bottom
  centre. The vertical half has a mechanism — DETR boxes include a little ground
  below the feet, and it was visible repeatedly while labelling. The horizontal
  half has no mechanism, and one possession is not enough to correct on. Both are
  folded into sigma instead of subtracted.
- **The grid had to be widened** from ±10 px to ±16 during labelling, because a
  box drawn tight round a running body puts its bottom centre further from a
  mid-stride foot than the first grid could show. That is most of why 3 px was so
  wrong.

This measures the foot-point term **only**. Total position error also carries M1's
calibration error (0.0996 yd mean on held-out correspondences); the two are close
to independent and add in quadrature to about 0.45 yd median.

## A calibration that disagreed with itself

Found while wiring the viewer, which is the first thing to use `H` directly rather
than re-deriving positions from the camera pose.

`H` put a detection's foot at field y = 16.914. The same detection's own `field`
said 23.713. The 6.8 yd gap is exactly the venue offset M2 corrected, from 25.95
to 32.75 — **M2's correction had been applied by editing `calibration.json` by
hand**, so the `venue_transform` block was updated and the 360 `H` matrices,
computed from the superseded offset, were not. `ur.detect.run` never noticed,
because it back-projects through the camera pose and applies the transform itself.
Detections were right all along; the contract was wrong.

Worse, `ur.calibrate.run` would have **silently reverted** the correction on any
re-run, because `main()` called `measure_transform` without the evidenced
near-sideline choice. A number that a re-run destroys is not a measurement.

Fixed in code: `venue.BREESE_STEVENS_NEAR_SIDELINE_Y`, overridable with
`--near-sideline`. `H` now agrees with all 5659 positioned detections to within
0.01 yd, and the M1 gate reproduces unchanged at 0.0996 yd mean, because the
acceptance measures in the soccer frame and a venue translation cannot touch it.

## The stand-in, and what it is not

`ur/standin.py` is greedy nearest-neighbour matching in field space, team-gated
and speed-gated, with no motion model, no Kalman filter, no global assignment and
no re-identification. It exists so M3 can be seen on screen. Its output carries
`stand_in: true` and a `notice` that says the slot identities should not be
quoted, the same way the fixture carries `synthetic: true`.

Measured on p0001:

| | |
|---|---|
| Observed fraction | 72.2 % (3640 of 5040 slot-frames) |
| Coverage per frame | median 11/14, min 0, max 14 |
| Slot claims outside the speed gate | **467** |
| Detections dropped for want of a free slot | 6 |

Those 467 are the honest headline. A detection no slot could claim inside its
speed gate is still a real measured position, so it takes a free slot anyway
rather than being thrown away — and that is where identity gets shuffled. It is
the clearest single reason this is a stand-in, and the count is written into
`possession.json` rather than left for someone to discover.

Coverage of 11/14 is below the ≈ 88 % M0 hand-counted for this possession. The
difference is detection misses, the 6.1 % of frames whose calibration confidence
is below 0.5 (which correctly produce no positions at all), and the deliberate
exclusion of `weak_team` detections from association — putting one of those in a
defender's slot would be inventing a defender.

**No `predicted`, no `interpolated`.** `docs/05` is explicit that those states
presume a motion model, and none exists before M4. An `unknown` sample carries
`xy: null` rather than a held-over position: the contract wants a sample for every
slot in every frame, and this provides one, but it refuses to provide a *number*.
Holding the last observed position while a sigma grows around it is dead reckoning
with the velocity set to zero.

## The viewer, and the sign that made it draw nothing

`viewer/live.html` plays `clip.mp4` behind an overlay canvas and draws the radar
beside it. It opens from `file://` with no build step; `tools/make_view.py` writes
`live-data.js`, because a `file://` page cannot `fetch` a sibling JSON.

Video↔frame mapping uses the relation ingest measured — `ceil((n + 0.5) ×
src_fps / track_fps) − 1` — not a uniform `n/15` clock. A silent one-frame offset
puts the overlay on the wrong moment with nothing on screen to signal it.

**The sign trap, worth keeping.** `H` is normalised so `H[2][2] = 1`, which fixes
the sign of the homogeneous `w` for image→field and leaves the *inverse's* overall
scale free. On this footage every point genuinely in front of the camera comes back
through `inv(H)` with **w negative**, so the obvious `w > 0` visibility test
rejects the entire field and the overlay draws nothing whatsoever. The reference
sign is now taken per frame from a point that must be in front — the bottom centre
of the image, pushed through `H` and straight back.

That failure was loud, which is the only reason it was cheap. Had the sign been
right and the *offset* wrong, the overlay would have drawn confidently in the
wrong place. `tools/m3_render.py` exists for that case: it is the only check that
exercises the field→image direction, which nothing else in the pipeline uses.

## Honest limits

- **The labels are the agent's own, not an independent human's.** On a 99 % gate
  over 234 items, two mistakes is the whole budget. The team labels disagree with
  `eval/m2/labels.json` on three frames and are not resolved in M2's favour;
  `eval/m3/team_labels.json` says why.
- **Foot-point reading noise is about ±1.5 px** on a clean crop and worse on the
  seven marked LOW CONFIDENCE. One sample is censored rather than dropped — on
  f205 #12 the box ends at the player's mid-back, so the true foot is off the grid
  and the reported maximum is a lower bound.
- **One possession, one camera, one lighting condition.** Nothing here says the
  kit fit or the stripe test generalises to daylight, to a different venue, or to
  the tight shots that make up a sixth of the broadcast.
- **The 120 vs 110 yd field length is still open** (`docs/08-risks.md` #5). It
  bounds only absolute distance along the field; matchups, separation and person
  vs zone are unaffected.
